| Inter-Service Rivalry between the Army and Navy | • In 1942, when the navy asked the army to provide troop support for an invasion of Australia, the army refused. <br>• The army remained unresponsive towards the navy throughout the Pacific war                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Overstretched Empire                            | • The Japanese empire was very spread out, and rail and road communications were poor in many areas<br>• Large quantities of raw materials and finished goods, such as weapons and vehicles, had to be transported by sea, which was a lot slower than through rail or road.                                                                                              |
| Poor Planning                                   | • The Japanese failed to understand that war at sea had changed by 1941, and that air power in the form of aircraft based on carriers was far more important that traditional naval power like battleships.<br>• The Japanese were unable to destroy any aircraft carriers during the Pearl Harbor attack, meaning that the US Navy could recover from the attack quickly |
| Lack of Local Support                           | • The Japanese abused the locals and forced thousands into slave labour, and millions of civilians died under Japanese rule.<br>• There were large-scale resistance movements.
