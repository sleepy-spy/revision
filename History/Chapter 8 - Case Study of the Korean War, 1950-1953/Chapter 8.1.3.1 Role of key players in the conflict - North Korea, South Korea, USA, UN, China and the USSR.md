# US and Soviet Disinterest
- On January 12, 1950, US Secretary of State Dean Acheson made a speech outlining the security policy of the US. In the speech he outlined the importance of Japan and the Philippines to the US, and US military forces would protect these areas. However, the Korean peninsula was not mentioned in this speech
- Stalin did not support Kim in supporting South Korea because he did not want a direct military confrontation with the United States
# The United States Shifts Its Position on Korea
- US State Department drew up the National Security Council Paper No. 68, meaning Truman would be inclined to intervene if war broke out
# Stalin and Mao Shift Their Position on Korea
- Kim persistently persuaded the leaders of the Soviet Union and China, Stalin and Mao, to support him in the attempt, and managed to change Stalin and Mao's mind about supporting an invasion of South Korea. 
# Course of the Korean War
## June-September 1950
- North Korean forces advanced rapid at the start and quickly capture Seoul, and were in control of most of South Korea
## September-Mid-October 1950
- UN forces landed in Incheon and quickly drove the North Koreans back, removing the North Koreans from South Korean, and advanced further towards the Yalu River.
## Mid-October 1950 - April 1951
- In October 1950, 200000 Chinese troops joined the North, and equipped with Soviet tanks and aircraft, they pushed the UN forces back into South Korea between December 1950 to January 1951
## July 1951 - July 1953
- The fighting reached a stalemate, armistice stalled due to disagreements over the terms, but was resumed and eventually signed at Panmunjom
