# Aftermath
- 3 to 4 million casualties on the Korean peninsula with around 1.4 million killed, with survivors becoming refugees
- Cities, towns and villages were flattened from fighting.
- 70 per cent of the textile industry, 70 per cent of the chemical industry and 51 per cent of the mining industry was destroyed
# Impact on South and North Korea
- 90000 South Korean POWs, 8000 UN POWs and 100000 North Korean POWs
- There was suspicion on both sides whether all POWs had been returned
- Many returning POWs were treated with suspicion because people feared they would be brainwashed
- Both troops moved their troops back two kilometres from the border, or the Military Demarcation Line, and the zone would be free of military presence
# Impact on US Foreign Policy
## Creation of Alliances and Escalation of Tensions
- United States stationed troops and significant air power and naval ships in South Korea and in Japan
- Extended its network of alliances beyond NATO. In 1954 the Southeast Asia Treaty Organisation in Southeast Asia and Central Treaty Organisation in Central Asia. Gave them money, advice and arms.
## Increase in Military Power
- The US armed forced doubled in size by 1951 compared to the previous year
- NATO countries also increased in military power. From 14 army divisions ad 5.5 per cent of their Gross Domestic Product on the military to 15 army divisions in West Germany alone and 12 per cent of their GDP on defence
