# The Tet Offensive, January 1968
- During the Vietnamese New Year holiday, Viet Cong fighters attacked over 100 cities and other military targets.
- The Viet Cong also captured the city of Hue for three weeks, and also fought bitterly with the US forces at the US military base at Khe Sanh
# Role of the Media
- The Tet Offensive convinced many journalists that the United States could not win. For example, Walter Cronkite of CBA News publicly criticised the US war effort in Vietnam on national television
# Rising Anti-War Sentiments
- The revelations and media coverage of the My Lai Massacre, in which US soldiers murdered between 347 and 504 unarmed South Vietnamese civilians, shocked the American public, leading to widespread protests
- Graphic images like the iconic "Saigon Execution" also fuelled the anti-war sentiments of mainstream media.
# United States Looks to Withdraw
- Richard Nixon began the policy of Vietnamisation, handling responsibility for the war to South Vietnamese forces and withdrawing 400000 US troops between 1969 and 1971.
- In January 1973, they signed the Paris Peace Accords to end the war and restore peace in Vietnam
# Fall of Saigon, April 1975
## Communist Advance
- In 1974, North Vietnamese launched a major military offensive against South Vietnam
- South Vietnamese President Nguyen Van Thieu resigned and fled to Saigon.
- Saigon fell, and the South Vietnamese government surrendered to the North Vietnamese forces
## Last Ditch Effort to Flee
- Televised images of desperate Vietnamese men, women and children trying to go aboard helicopters taking off from the US embassy in Saigon as part of Operation Frequent Wind, the evacuation of US civilians and Vietnamese in 1975
# Communist Victory.
- Communist government established iron-fisted rule, rounding up hundreds of thousands of South Vietnamese and put them in prison camps, and were subjected to indoctrination, torture and forced labour
- Under Communist Party Chairman Le Duan, they aligned itself closely with the USSR, and continued to receive aid to secure and control the new nation
# Aftermath of the Vietnam War
## Bankrupt Ideology
- The Vietnam War demonstrated that the ideology of the West was not morally superior to communism. The atrocities committed by US soldiers and use of chemical weapons also damaged US reputation
# Failure of Containment
- After the fall of South Vietnam, Laos and Cambodia also turned communist by 1975
## Weakening of the US Presidency
- The 1973 War Powers Act limited the ability of the president to go to war
# Detente
- The United States and USSR signed the Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty to reduce nuclear weapons, and were a result of successful bilateral negotiations.
