# Local Agents: The Viet Cong and the South Vietnamese Government
- The Viet Cong used the Ho Chi Minh Trail to send reinforcements and supplies to 40000 North Vietnamese Army troops, who were guerrilla fighters, between 1961 and 1963.
- Attacked South Vietnamese government forces, officials and buildings, avoiding well-defended South Vietnamese positions and focused on making the countryside unsafe for government forces
- South Vietnam responded with the Strategic Hamlet programme, where peasants were relocated, sometimes by force, to fortified villages away from the control and influence of the Viet Cong
- Strategic Hamlet also supported Diem's vision of transforming the countryside
# External Players
## Involvement of China
- Mao Zedong eager to support any state or group that created anti-US buffer in Vietnam, especially if it was communist
## Involvement of USSR
- USSR was not active in Asia in the early 1960s, because the relations between the USSR and China had deteriorated, and the two communist powers were less keen to cooperate
## Involvement of the United States
- Kennedy sent many military advisors to train the South vietnamese Army, and continued supplying military equipment. These efforts were organised under the Military Assistance Command Vietnam
