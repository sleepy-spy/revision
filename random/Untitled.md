A **Diophantine equation** is a fundamental topic in number theory, which is the branch of mathematics concerned with the properties of integers.

  

In simple terms, a Diophantine equation is an equation for which **only integer solutions** are sought.

  

---

  

### 1. Formal Definition

  

A Diophantine equation is an algebraic equation, typically involving polynomials, where the goal is to find solutions $(x, y, z, \dots)$ that are restricted to be integers ($\mathbb{Z}$).

  

The classic form is:

$$a x + b y = c$$

where $a$, $b$, and $c$ are given integers, and we are looking for integer values for $x$ and $y$.

  

### 2. The Core Constraint

  

The defining characteristic of a Diophantine equation is the constraint: **the variables must be integers.**

  

*   **Contrast with standard algebra:** In regular algebra, you can have real number solutions (e.g., $x=1.5, y=3.33$). In Diophantine equations, we are only concerned with solutions where $x, y$ are whole numbers ($\dots, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, \dots$).

  

### 3. Historical Context

  

The term was introduced by the Greek mathematician **Diophantus of Alexandria** in the 3rd century AD. He studied these problems, and his work laid the foundation for the modern study of number theory.

  

### 4. Examples

  

**Simple Linear Example:**

Find all integer solutions to the equation:

$$2x + 3y = 10$$

*   We can test integers: If $x=2$, then $2(2) + 3y = 10 \implies 4 + 3y = 10 \implies 3y = 6 \implies y=2$.

*   So, $(x=2, y=2)$ is an integer solution.

*   If $x=5$, then $2(5) + 3y = 10 \implies 10 + 3y = 10 \implies 3y = 0 \implies y=0$.

*   The set of integer solutions is infinite.

  

**Non-Linear Example:**

$$x^2 + y^2 = z^2$$

This is the classic **Pythagorean theorem** equation. Finding Diophantine solutions means finding sets of integers $(x, y, z)$ that satisfy this relationship (these are known as Pythagorean triples, like (3, 4, 5)).

  

### 5. Mathematical Significance

  

The study of Diophantine equations is immensely important because:

  

1.  **It bridges algebra and number theory:** It combines the structure of algebra with the properties of integers.

2.  **Difficulty:** Unlike standard algebraic equations (where solutions can often be found using continuous methods), finding integer solutions is often very difficult. It requires specialized techniques from number theory, modular 

arithmetic, and advanced factorization.

3.  **Foundation of Modern Math:** The study of these equations led to major breakthroughs in the development of number theory and the understanding of prime numbers.
