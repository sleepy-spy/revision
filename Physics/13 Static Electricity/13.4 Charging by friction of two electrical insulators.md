# How it occurs
- Due to friction, electrons may be transferred from electrically neutral insulator to another electrically neutral insulator when they rub against each other
- Only atoms on the surface lose and gain electrons 
	- Surface atoms for the electron transferrer becomes cations, and surface atoms for the electron receiver becomes anions
# Can electrically neutral conductors be charged friction?
- No, any excess charges on the conductors are quickly neutralised

| - Charged                                                                                                                                                                              | + Charged                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Excess electrons in the first conductor are repelled into the second conductor<br><br>Excess electrons move through the hand and into the earth<br><br>First conductor becomes neutral | Free electrons in the second conductor are attracted by, move to and neutralise the positive charges on the first conductor.<br><br>Free electrons in the earth are also attracted by, move through the hand, and neutralise the positive charges on the first conductor<br><br>First conductor becomes neutral |
