## Ionising Radiation
- Ionising radiation is radiation that has enough energy to ionize atoms or molecules by removing electrons from them
- High energy electromagnetic waves like ultraviolet radiation, x-rays and gamma rays can ionize atoms and molecules
- Exposure to ionising radiation can damage biological tissue and lead to
	- Abnormal cell division
	- Deformities in developing foetus
	- Cancers
	- Premature ageing
	- Shortening of lifespan
## Infrared Radiation
- A barbecue pit is a net emitter of infrared radiation. When we stand near a barbecue pit, our bodies absorb the infrared radiation from it, allowing us to feel warm
- Heat transfer by radiation
	- All objects emit electromagnetic waves
	- Objects that have a temperature of between -200$\degree\mathrm{C}$ and a few thousand $\degree\mathrm{C}$ emi primarily infrared radiation
	- As the temperature of the object increases, the intensity of the infrared radiation increases, and the frequency of the emitted infrared radiation increases, and the wavelength of it decreases
