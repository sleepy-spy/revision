$$\mathrm{speed\ of\ sound} = \frac{\text{1-way distance}}{\text{1-way time}}=\frac{\text{2-way distance}}{\text{2-way time}}$$
## Practical Applications
- Animals like bats and dolphins use echolocations to locate objects and prey around them
- Boats use sonar technology in order to determine the depth of the seabed
	- Since some energy is also transferred to the internal stores of the water and the seabed, through the seabed, and in directions other than toward the receiver, the amplitude of the reflected pulse received by the boat is lower than the one that was sent out
- In quality control, ultrasound can be used to locate cracks and cavities in concrete slabs and metal pipes
	- After an emitter emits ultrasound, the ultrasound reflects off the crack and is detected by the receiver
- Medical imaging
	- Ultrasound pulses are incident on surfaces of organs or foetuses that are different distances away from the transmitter
	- Some energy from the pulse that was produced by the transmitter is reflected back into the receiver, while some of the energy was transferred to the internal stores of the foetus and body, and some energy is also transmitted through the surface because of refraction
	- A computer processes the different time intervals between the transmitted and received pulses and the different amplitudes and directions of the received pulse are measured, and displays an image
	- Unlike low frequency sound, high frequency sound can be manipulated into thin beams, just like laser beams
	- X-rays are **not** used in pre-natal scanning because X-rays emit ionizing radiation which could kill the cells and lead to deformities in the foetus, or cause mutations. In contrast, ultrasound has lower energy and is thus less hazardous
