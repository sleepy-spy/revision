## Definition
- Reflection is light being bounced off after hitting an opaque surface
## Common terms
![[Screenshot 2026-02-15 at 6.51.22 PM.png]]
- The incident ray is the light ray coming to the surface
- The reflected ray is the light after reflecting off the surface
- The normal is an imaginary straight line that is perpendicular (90°) to the surface of the mirror.
- The point of incidence P is the point where the incident ray, reflected ray and the normal meet.
- The angle of incidence (i) is the angle between the incident ray and the normal.
- The angle of reflection (r) is the angle between the reflected ray and the normal.
## Laws of Reflection
- First Law
	- The first law of reflection states that the angle of incidence, angle of reflection and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane
- Second Law
	- The second law of reflection states that the incident ray, i, is equal to the reflected ray, r
## Characteristics of Image formed by a Plane Mirror
- It is laterally inverted
- It is the same shape and the same size as the object
- It is upright
- It is virtual
	- Virtual images cannot be projected on a screen, and are formed by divergent rays
	- Real light rays do not meet at the position
- Its image distance from the mirror is the same as the object distance from the mirror
## Types of Reflection
### Regular Reflection
- On smooth surfaces, reflection is regular.
- Parallel incident rays have the same angles of incidence and reflection
- The reflected rays travel in the same direction
### Irregular Reflection
- On irregular surfaces, reflection is diffused
- The normal at different points of the surface are not parallel to one another
- Thus, even if the incident rays were parallel, they have different angles of incidence and reflection. The reflected rays travel in **different directions**
- The overall diffused image is made up of many image points at different locations
